# Murdoku

🌐 **Play online:** [osdgdt.github.io/murdoku](https://osdgdt.github.io/murdoku/) — nothing to install, it runs in the browser (phone or desktop) and can be installed as an app.

Murdoku is a hybrid of Sudoku and detective deduction. People are placed on a grid, **one per row and column at most**, following clues tied to a map of rooms and objects. The victim is placed too, and one rule always holds: **the victim is in a room with exactly one other person — the murderer.**

A **medieval / dark-fantasy pack** adds 17 floors (dark flagstones, runed stone, packed earth, mud, straw, ossuary, bridge planks, red damask, decorated tiles…) and 25 props in a "Medieval and fantasy" group of the editor palette: beds, benches, thrones, bearskin rugs and straw pallets can be occupied (benches and thrones are seats); barrels, chests, fireplaces, anvils, altars, statues, armor, weapon racks, cauldrons, wells, carts, sarcophagi, lecterns, banners, candelabra, cages, gravestones and crystal balls block the cell; portcullises and arrow slits sit on the edge like doors and windows. There are also 10 **medieval characters** (blacksmith, executioner, herald, thief, alchemist, archer, princess, prince, jester, bard) next to the king, queen, knight, monk, wizard and countess.

After a site update, a "A new version of the site is available" banner offers a reload: the service worker (`sw.js`) installs the new files bypassing the browser's HTTP cache (GitHub Pages keeps it for 10 minutes), and `npm run e2e:update` simulates a deploy to check it.

A room's **colour** can be changed too: pick the room and use the "Colour" row below its name: 16 named colours (terracotta, dusty blue, sage, mustard… plus red, lime, pink, cyan, blue, sand, brown, slate) and a "Custom" picker. The chip shows the colour the map will really show; the legend, the room list and the map follow by themselves, one undo step per change. Custom colours are softened by the paper skin to match the rest (same hue, the palette's saturation and lightness).

Rooms can be **renamed** in the editor: pick a room (click its coloured circle) and change its name in the "Rename room" field (Enter or click away to confirm; up to 40 characters; a blank name is refused). The new name shows at once on the map, legend, clues and print view; the id, colour, floor and cells are unchanged, so clues are untouched; one undo step per change.

The app is available in **Italian and English** (IT/EN button, top right; it follows your browser language by default). Puzzle text written by an author (title, briefing, names, room names) stays as authored.

## How to play

1. Pick a case from the player (or import a `.murdoku.json` file) and start from the toolbar.
2. Pick a character from the toolbar. **Tap a cell** to jot a note (a candidate); **press and hold** a cell to place the character there; **press, hold and drag** to mark several cells at once. Placing someone crosses out the rest of their row and column.
3. **Click a character** in the toolbar to light up, in gold, every cell their clues point at (a whole room, every plant…); hovering shows only the cells that fit all their clues together.
4. Stuck? **💡 Hint** explains a certain deduction from the clues (never from a stored answer) and highlights the cell; use ‹ › to move through the chain.
5. **✓ Check solution** only says whether the case is solved — it never reveals how many placements are right.
6. After solving you can **📤 share your result** (title, time, hints — no spoilers).

The **tutorial** (`tutorial.html`, “How to play”, linked from the home page and under the board while playing) teaches the rules by solving a small original case step by step, then covers the objects, three strategy tricks and the app's gestures. The case and its deduction are computed with the solver's own rules (`src/tutorial/tutorialCase.js`), and a test keeps the page's story true.

## Create and share cases

- **Clue glossary:** `glossary.html` (linked from the home page and under the board while playing) explains every clue type in plain words, with the sentence as the game words it and a mini board: for clues about a character the green cells are where the clue holds; general clues show the same scene once true and once false. The examples are real little cases judged by the solver's own rules, so they can't drift from the game.
- **Reasonings and difficulty (`src/solver/techniques/`):** besides the exact search (which stays the judge of uniqueness) there is a **“human” solver** that solves a case like a person, one reasoning at a time, always with the easiest one that works, and says which ones it needed. The reasonings live in a **catalogue by level** (`catalog.js`, one line to move one): _Easy_ (clue filter, row and column taken, only candidate), _Medium_ (clue with a known reference, scene clue that rules out cells, room for two, the victim rule, counting in rooms, last cell of a row/column, forced row or column, groups of characters, clue with one other open character), _Hard_ (rows or columns of a group, clues between three open characters, “or” clues, clues about the whole scene, complete arrangements when few characters are left) and _Expert_ (hypothesis and contradiction, nested hypotheses, search). **The difficulty of a case is the hardest reasoning it cannot do without.** It shows in the editor's difficulty line (with an expandable detail: which reasonings, how many times, from which step, and **the board of the human step by step**: notes, X's and placements after every deduction, with ◀ ▶, jumps to the next reasoning or placement and automatic play; by default only the notes of the character of the step are drawn, “Show everybody's notes” draws them all; `src/solver/techniques/replay.js` and `src/editor/replayView.js`), the ✨ Propose generator builds the set until the case is **exactly** the level asked for (otherwise it says which level it came out), the player's hints name the reasoning behind each deduction, and the **Glossary** has a “Reasonings” section with, for each, a worded example and a **real mini board**: a tiny case (`src/glossary/reasoningWorlds.js`, found by `tools/find-reasoning-examples.mjs`) on which the human solver really makes that step, showing the cells that remain, the ones ruled out and the clues used (`test/test-glossary.js` checks that every example still shows its reasoning). The difficulty declared by the author never changes by itself. The tutorial is a special case: it is introductory, so it stays “very easy” even though one of its clues needs a Medium reasoning. `node tools/calibrate-techniques.mjs` measures the level of cases and of generated sets (`--generate`); `npm run bench` also measures the human solver on the editor and test cases (level, hardest reasoning, work); `test/test-human-solver.js` checks that the human solver never rules out a cell of the solution, comparing it with the exact search.
- **Editor:** build the map (rooms with ~25 floors, furniture, doors and windows as pieces of wall shared by the two cells they lie between, blocked cells; grids from 3×3 up to **16×16**, with one character per row and column), add characters (~55 professions to pick from), and give each character clues from 40+ clue types (any clue can be negated, combined with "either/or", or be a general fact about the scene; "is in front of a window" covers the two cells a window separates; "is the only person who…" says the character has a trait (sitting on a chair, being a man, being next to something, or any other condition) and nobody else on the map does; the general clue "exactly N people have this trait" ("exactly 2 people are sitting on a chair", "exactly 3 women"; negated, "it is not true that…") counts them across the whole map; "is next to a wall" holds when any side of the cell is a wall (between two rooms, between a room and the outside or a blocked cell, or the border of the map; on a skyscraper, of the floor); "is next to a room" means touching one of its walls from outside; you can sit on chairs and stools, not on rugs). One menu sets the **verb tense** of every clue in the case at once (present "is next to…", past "was next to…", future "will be next to…"), and the wording is always "next to", never "near". The old obsolete clue types are gone: old cases are converted automatically on load. **Writing a clue is a guided two-step flow** (a side drawer on a PC, a bottom sheet on a phone): first _what do you want to say_ (plain-language families, with search), then _complete the sentence_ with one control per part; targets come from grouped lists or by **tapping the board**; the board previews what the clue points at and a ✓/✗ says whether it is true in the solution you declared. Clues sit in a compact list under the board (edit, change type, duplicate, move, remove; incomplete ones are flagged), and **💡 Suggest** lists the clues that are true in your solution, one tap to add. **✨ Propose** composes a whole set of clues that makes your declared solution the only one (Easy/Medium/Hard level; by default no clue about the victim, starting from the clues already there: you tick the ones to keep and add them in one undoable step; `src/solver/composer.js`, the same core as the library builder). **⬇ Export clues / ⬆ Import clues** carry a set of clues from one case to another (`.murdoku-clues.json`: characters and rooms by name, furniture by type and place; on import equal names link by themselves, you fix the rest by hand, and clues that cannot be placed are listed with the reason, never half-added). A line above the list shows, after every edit, **how many solutions are left, the estimated difficulty and how many characters plain deduction pins down at once** (it replaces the old “Estimate difficulty” button; “limit reached” is never written as “no solution”). The link holds for objects too: whoever stands on one end is “next to the lift” (or the stairs) of the other floors, while a piece of furniture on another floor is never next to anything. - **Big grids (up to 16×16):** cells shrink to 40px (56px up to 9 columns) and the board scrolls inside its area; on a phone the **✋ Move** button (shown only when the board is wider than the screen) drags the board with a finger instead of marking cells. With 10-16 characters the notes are a 4×4 box per cell, and where the cell is small (under ~50px) they become plain coloured dots in the character's fixed slot. Keys: 1-9 and 0 pick the first ten characters, **[** and **]** (or **,** and **.**) step to the previous/next one; there are 16 character colours (the first 10 unchanged). Printing shrinks cells to 34px beyond 12 columns to fit A4. A well-clued 16×16 case needs about 3-4 clues per character.
- **📅 Daily challenge** (`daily.html`): one case a day, **the same for everybody** (the library's “case of the day”, chosen by the UTC date), played **with no hints**; the time counts while the page is open and visible, with one scored game per account per day (Google sign-in, like campaigns). You choose a **nickname** (3-20 characters, unique ignoring case: it is the only name shown) and the result goes on the **board of the day** (and yesterday's): time first, then fewer wrong checks. Times are reported by the browser and no server checks the solution: a board for fun, not a proof (the Privacy page says so too). The nickname and games can be deleted from “Privacy”. **The challenge only exists if the library has cases** (`examples/library.json`, empty today: add cases with `tools/make-library.mjs` or by hand); with none the page says “No challenge today”. Data model and rules: `src/daily/` and the “Daily challenge” section of `firestore.rules`.
- **Accessibility:** the game can be played **with the keyboard only and with a screen reader**: the board is a single tab stop (arrows, Home/End, PageUp/PageDown, Ctrl+Home/End; Enter = note, Shift+Enter = place), every cell says what it holds, focus is kept after each move, and every move, hint, check and the win is announced (`aria-live` regions, `src/util/announcer.js`). Three themes: light, dark and **high contrast** (black on white, highlights told apart by shape, characters recognised by icon and initial rather than colour only); the theme button cycles them and, when nothing was chosen, the device decides (`prefers-contrast: more`, `forced-colors`). Slow work (hints, the answer check) shows elapsed time, a bar and **Cancel**, and in a hidden tab the title says when the result is ready. Contrast ratios are checked by `test/test-contrast.js`; trying it with a real screen reader is in `docs/accessibility-checklist.md`.
- **Campaigns** (sign in with Google): ordered sequences of cases with progress synced across devices. Only the site owner can create them. For story campaigns, any clue of a case can be **hidden until an earlier case is solved** (a padlock stands in for it; the solution is still checked against the complete case) — so each case can hand a piece of evidence to the finale. Each case can also carry an **introduction** (read before the case, with a “Start the case” button, and readable again afterwards), an **epilogue** (shown when the case is solved) and the campaign can end with a **finale**: a question and two endings the player chooses from after the last case. The texts are the author's own, one language per campaign, always shown as plain text; a blank line separates paragraphs. A complete example (plot, case sheets, ready-to-paste texts) is in `docs/campaign-story.en.md`.

**Statistics:** the game keeps on the device the **effective playing time** (counted only while the page is visible, and carried over reloads), hints, wrong checks and cases begun or left unfinished (see “Your history”). For campaign cases, a signed-in player adds to the global totals once per case (`caseStats/{campaign__case}` in Firestore: players, solves, time, hints, wrong checks) and the case list shows “average time · hints · % finish it · N players” from the third player on. The numbers are self-reported, so indicative; **republish `firestore.rules`** for the global statistics to work — without it the game carries on and the statistics simply do not appear. **The rules are tested against the emulator:** `cd test/rules && npm install` (once; needs Java) then `npm run test:rules` from the root (`REQUIRE_RULES=1` makes it mandatory, otherwise it skips with a message); `test/rules/rules.test.mjs` covers the case statistics, nicknames and the daily challenge (it accepts the valid operations and refuses exceeded bounds, a second submission, the wrong day, someone else's nickname…). From the repository, `firebase.json` names the rules file, so `firebase deploy --only firestore:rules --project murdoku-61284` is enough. The rules let `caseStats/{key}` be read by key only (never listed) and written only together with the player's own marker `users/{uid}/caseStats/{key}`, with bounded increments; try them in the Rules Playground or the Firebase emulator.

Campaign admins: the original owner's uid stays hard-coded in `firestore.rules` (so a misconfiguration can never lock the owner out); to add another admin, create a document `admins/<uid>` by hand in the Firebase console (clients cannot write it), then publish `firestore.rules`.

Everything except campaigns works without an account, and your cases/progress live in your browser's local storage.

## Development

A static site with ES modules and **no build step**; `package.json` only holds dev tooling.

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000 (on Windows, double-click `Avvia Murdoku.bat`: same server on port 8123, opens the browser)
npm install
npm test        # unit tests under Node (+ checks that sw.js is up to date)
npm run lint
npm run format  # Prettier check (use `npx prettier --write` to fix)
npm run i18n    # strings still missing an English translation
npm run e2e     # Playwright: plays a fixture case (`test/fixtures`) in IT and EN
npm run e2e:all # + accessibility (axe), editor and gameplay gestures
npm run check:size  # no tracked file over 1 MB, plus a cap per kind (src JS 64 KB, CSS 48 KB, HTML 40 KB, images 150 KB, visual references 500 KB in all); prints the 10 biggest files
npm run e2e:visual:docker  # pixel comparison of key screens (16×16 board light/dark/high contrast, the clue wizard on desktop and phone, the clue list) with the references in `test/visual/`, **inside Playwright's Docker image** (the same one the CI `visual` job uses: same fonts and renderer everywhere); `-- --update` rewrites the references (look at them before committing). Needs Docker; the CI does not. Not part of `e2e:all`: it runs in its own job, never on `main`, so a pixel difference can never stop `main` from being published
npm run e2e:touch   # the phone with real touch events (CDP): the 16×16 board (a finger that scrolls, a note, a long press, «Place», «Reserve», a long press on a row label) and the clue wizard (open, pick a type, pick a target by tapping the board, scroll the sheet, targets ≥ 44 px)
npm run e2e:i18n    # in English each page loads only the dictionary areas it needs and shows English
npm run i18n        # strings missing in English + a page-by-page check that no string a page uses sits in an area the page does not load
npm run check:case -- case.murdoku.json [other.murdoku.json]  # checks a case before it goes into the library (or the daily challenge): one solution, the declared solution agrees with the clues, superfluous clues, declared vs estimated difficulty, the house rules of library cases; given the `.it.` and `.en.` files of one case it also checks they are the same case. `--library` checks the whole library. Exits 1 on an error (warnings do not fail)
```

The English dictionary is split by area (`src/i18n/en/*.js`) and **each page loads only its own** (`src/i18n/areas.js`: e.g. the glossary strings only on the glossary and the editor, the tutorial's only on the tutorial): an English visitor downloads about half the dictionary on the common pages, and Italian visitors load none. `npm run i18n` and a test check, page by page, that no string a page uses ends up in an area it does not load (shared strings live in `ui.js`).

Layout: `src/model` (data, clue types, migrations) · `src/solver` (predicates, propagation, hints, validator — runs in a Web Worker) · `src/editor`, `src/player`, `src/campaign` (UI) · `src/storage` (localStorage, import/export, share links) · `src/i18n` (translations) · `examples/` (bundled cases) · `sw.js` + `manifest.webmanifest` (PWA).

Imported cases (file, `?import=<url>`, share link) are untrusted input: they are size-capped, migrated to the current schema and validated (safe ids and colors only) before anything is rendered.
