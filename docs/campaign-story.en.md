# The King Is Dead — the campaign story

> **Spoiler warning.** This document reveals the culprit of every case, the finale included. Do not share it with anyone who is going to play the campaign. Italian version: [campagna-storia.md](campagna-storia.md).

Ten cases of rising difficulty, a dark tone, no magic. The player is the **court investigator** of the kingdom of **Carvenna**. Cases 1 to 9 are murders of young adults; the tenth, on a 9×9 grid, is the death of the king and is solved with the evidence gathered in the nine before it (hidden clues, see the campaigns section of `README.en.md`).

Names, places and details are proposals: change them freely, as long as they respect the rules at the end (initials, no V among the suspects). The English version uses English-sounding names of its own, not word-for-word translations of the Italian ones.

---

## 1. Premise

**The kingdom.** Carvenna: an invented, generically European realm, with a castle on a wind-beaten crag (**Hollowmere Keep**), a town of forges and smoke at its foot (**Cinderwick**), an abbey (Saint Hilary's) and a court full of offices and grudges.

**The royal couple.** King **Victor**, 58, vain enough to put his own name or initial on everything. Queen **Adela**, 54, calm, devout, a great connoisseur of herbs and gardens.

**The lost son.** Their only legitimate son, **Edward**, died of a fever at nine, twenty-four years ago. Adela chose the name; Victor never forgave her (it didn't start with a V). From that day the king stopped loving her and began to cheat on her, in secret, with women of every rank.

**The illegitimate children.** Nine, born over the following twenty years of different women. The king, an egotist, gave them all names starting with V. They are now between 18 and 23, of age, and with no legitimate heir each one hopes (or is pushed to hope) for the throne.

**The king's death.** One autumn night Victor drinks his evening cup and goes to bed. The next morning he is found dead in one of the two beds of the marital chamber. The coroner, the court physician **Eustace**, declares that «the heart gave out». In truth Adela, who had found out about the affairs, had been poisoning him for months with small doses of foxglove, and has bought Eustace's silence.

**The rumours.** The king was healthy. The court whispers about poison. Adela cannot pretend not to hear: to control the inquiry she **appoints an investigator herself**, one she believes she can steer. That is the player.

**The children's secret.** As soon as the king dies, the illegitimate children move. Each, for reasons of their own (to prove the crime, to gain an edge, to frighten a rival), quietly gathers **one testimony about one person** on the night of the death and hides it in an object their father had given them. None of them shares anything with the others.

**The deaths.** While the kingdom mourns, the children begin to die. Every death has a different culprit and a motive of its own, and none has to do with the queen. The court devours its own heirs: that is the tone, bitter and without heroes.

**The surprise.** When a case is solved, the investigator finds the object and the hidden testimony. In the tenth case the nine testimonies, put together, show who could have been in the king's chamber: and the truth is the person who chose you.

## 2. The finale's cast

Eight suspects plus the victim (the 9×9 finale has nine people). All initials differ from one another (on the player's notes the first letter identifies a character) and none is V.

| Initial | Name | Who | Why suspected |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Adela** | the queen | the betrayed wife, and the one who finds him first |
| M | **Matilda** | noblewoman, a count's widow; mother of 3 of the king's children | broken promises of marriage |
| G | **Gwen** | innkeeper of Cinderwick; mother of 2 children | the king left her with the debts |
| B | **Bertha** | seamstress, former novice; mother of 2 children | expelled from the abbey because of him |
| R | **Roger** | chancellor of the realm | fears losing power if the throne changes hands |
| E | **Eustace** | court physician and coroner | signed a false report |
| W | **Wat** | the king's cupbearer | brings the evening cup |
| C | **Cecily** | the queen's chambermaid | goes in and out of the marital chamber |
| — | **Victor** | the king, the victim | |

The two children whose mothers are not in the cast have mothers who died or went far away.

The king slept in one of the **two beds** of the marital chamber; the other is the queen's. A room with two beds and the medieval objects already available (bed, fireplace, chest, candelabrum, altar, cauldron, barrel, well, armor, banner, throne) are enough for the whole castle map.

## 3. How the evidence is gathered

Case *n* (1 to 9) ends with the discovery of the object the victim kept: inside is testimony *n*. In case 10 that testimony becomes **a hidden clue**: in the campaign editor, for each clue of case 10 choose «unlocked by solving case *n*».

| Case | Kept object | Testimony | About | Clue idea for case 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | hollow iron token | «The cupbearer never went into the chamber: he left the cup on the anteroom table and went back to the pantry.» | Wat | Wat is next to a barrel (pantry) |
| 2 | herbal with pressed leaves | «The chambermaid sat mending in front of the anteroom fireplace.» | Cecily | Cecily is in front of the anteroom fireplace |
| 3 | hollow silver quill | «The physician was praying in the chapel, by the altar, while the king died.» | Eustace | Eustace is next to the altar |
| 4 | soldier's medal | «The seamstress was sewing in the corridor, near the armor.» | Bertha | Bertha is next to the armor |
| 5 | pewter tankard with a false bottom | «The innkeeper was in the courtyard, by the well, unloading wine.» | Gwen | Gwen is next to the well |
| 6 | rosary | «The noblewoman was waiting for an audience in the council hall, in front of the throne.» | Matilda | Matilda is in front of the throne |
| 7 | silver spoon (hollow handle) | «The chancellor was writing in the anteroom, near the candelabrum.» | Roger | Roger is next to the candelabrum |
| 8 | ring of keys | «The queen prayed in the chapel before supper.» (a *neutral* clue: it looks irrelevant) | Adela | Adela is in the chapel (before, not at that hour) |
| 9 | signet ring | «The king slept in the bed by the window; the one by the fireplace was the queen's.» | the king's bed | Victor is in the window bed |

These are ideas: adapt them to the map you draw. The only rule that matters: in the unique solution of case 10, **only Adela can be alone with the king** at the hour of the crime, yet the testimonies, one at a time, seem to be about other people.

**Tips for case 10.**
- Everyone has access and a motive: leave several «ordinary» clues (visible from the start) that seem to point at Roger or Eustace.
- The clue about the king's bed (case 9) is the one that closes the circle: keep it among the last.
- Measured so far: with 8 suspects a unique solution needs about 12-14 personal clues; nine come from the earlier cases.

## 4. The queen's part in cases 1-9

Adela is never among the suspects of cases 1-9, but she appears in every introduction and every epilogue as the investigator's **patron**: she receives them, opens doors, thanks them. Small tells, to sow with discretion (you do not need them all):

1. Case 1: she says she knew nothing about the king's children. (She did.)
2. Case 2: she grows foxglove in her garden and mentions it without embarrassment.
3. Case 3: she advises you not to waste time on the court physician, «an honest man».
4. Case 5: she knows a detail of the scene you had not told her.
5. Case 7: she offers you a cup, and drinks first herself.
6. Case 8: it is she who reminds you the pretenders are almost gone.
7. Case 9: she weeps, but smiles when she turns away.

## 5. The case sheets

Grid, characters and objects are suggestions. **No suspect** may have a name starting with V (V is reserved for King Victor and his children). Within one case, all initials differ.

### Case 1 — The Smith's Apprentice · 4×4
- **Victim**: Vance, 19, a smith's apprentice.
- **Scene**: the forge in Cinderwick. Objects: anvil, fireplace (the furnace), barrel, weapon rack.
- **Culprit**: Walter, the master smith. **Motive**: Vance had found out he sold flawed blades to the garrison.
- **Other suspects** (3 in all, culprit included): Hob (journeyman), Maud (the smith's wife).
- **Object**: a hollow iron token bearing Victor's crest; it shows that Vance was the king's son.

**Introduction.** Queen Adela has picked you to find out how her husband died, but to begin with she sends you to Cinderwick: a smith's apprentice lies dead beside the anvil. «A small matter,» she says, «but an investigation starts wherever it can.» The forge is still hot, and three people swear they saw nothing.

**Epilogue.** The master smith confesses: Vance had discovered the flawed blades sold to the army. In the iron token the boy wore round his neck there is Victor's crest, and a slip: «The cupbearer never went into the chamber». You tell the queen the apprentice was the king's son. «I did not know,» Adela whispers.

### Case 2 — The Abbey Garden · 4×4
- **Victim**: Viola, 20, an herbalist.
- **Scene**: the garden and infirmary of Saint Hilary's. Objects: well, bench, statue, cauldron.
- **Culprit**: Brother Anselm, the infirmarian. **Motive**: Viola had found his ledger of poisons, which he sold in secret.
- **Other suspects** (3 in all): Sister Clemence, Gerard (gardener).
- **Object**: a herbal with pressed leaves.

**Introduction.** At Saint Hilary's Abbey a young herbalist has been found dead in the garden, among the beds. The queen has written to you: «My own garden is full of the same plants; let me know if anyone is misusing them.» The abbot receives you through clenched teeth.

**Epilogue.** Brother Anselm gives way when you show him his ledger of poisons: he had been selling them to the nobles of the town. In Viola's herbal, among the foxglove leaves, a note: «The chambermaid sat mending in front of the fireplace». Two of the king's children dead in a few days: you already know they will not be the last.

### Case 3 — The Scriptorium · 5×5
- **Victim**: Valerian, 22, a scribe.
- **Scene**: the abbey's scriptorium. Objects: lectern, chest, candelabrum, bench.
- **Culprit**: Ambrose, the abbot's secretary. **Motive**: he forged title deeds; Valerian had noticed.
- **Other suspects** (4 in all): Benedict (copyist), Theobald (illuminator), Perpetua (the librarian nun).
- **Object**: a hollow silver quill.

**Introduction.** A copyist has been killed in the scriptorium, his quill still on the page. The queen has you told to be discreet: «A monastery's papers are delicate, and many trust them more than the king.» Four suspects, and a single account book in disorder.

**Epilogue.** Ambrose had forged some thirty deeds and Valerian had caught him. Inside the silver quill, a slip: «The physician was praying in the chapel while the king died». The court physician, the queen warns you, is an honest man: do not waste your time.

### Case 4 — The Three Ravens · 5×5
- **Victim**: Vaughan, 21, a mercenary.
- **Scene**: the tavern in Cinderwick. Objects: barrels, fireplace, benches.
- **Culprit**: Rudolf One-Eye, a sellsword. **Motive**: a gambling debt, and Vaughan had caught him cheating at dice.
- **Other suspects** (4 in all): Godric (merchant), Ursula (serving girl), Drogo (drover).
- **Object**: a soldier's medal.

**Introduction.** At the Three Ravens a rumour has spread, and so has a corpse: a mercenary, laid out among the benches with a dagger in his side. «There they gamble, drink, say anything,» the queen lets you know. «Listen to everything, and trust no one.»

**Epilogue.** Rudolf admits it all, with a bitter laugh. Under Vaughan's medal, a slip: «The seamstress was sewing in the corridor, near the armor». The queen nods softly when you tell her: «Another young life wasted».

### Case 5 — The Royal Stables · 6×6
- **Victim**: Verity, 21, the court's stable hand.
- **Scene**: the stables and the tournament field. Objects: cart, banner, weapon rack, bench.
- **Culprit**: Sir Aldous, a knight. **Motive**: Verity had seen him rig his lance at the tourneys.
- **Other suspects** (5 in all): Sigurd (squire), Brant (stable boy), Isolde (lady), Ermengarde (wardrobe mistress).
- **Object**: a pewter tankard with a false bottom.

**Introduction.** At the edge of the tournament field they have found a stable hand, killed among the stalls. The queen already knows some details of the scene and apologises for it: «The stablemen talk to me.» You note it down and move on.

**Epilogue.** Sir Aldous falls to your simplest argument: the rigged lance. In the false bottom of Verity's tankard, a note: «The innkeeper was in the courtyard, by the well». Five of the king's children now, and all of them dead.

### Case 6 — The Crypt · 6×6
- **Victim**: Vesper, 20, a novice.
- **Scene**: the abbey's crypt. Objects: altar, sarcophagus, gravestone, candelabrum.
- **Culprit**: Osbert, the sacristan. **Motive**: he stole the silver of the reliquaries; Vesper saw him.
- **Other suspects** (5 in all): Abbot Leofwin, Reynold (gravedigger), Eleanor (pilgrim), Martin (pilgrim).
- **Object**: a rosary.

**Introduction.** In the abbey crypt, where the king will be buried, a novice has been found at the foot of a sarcophagus. The queen will come for the funeral rites and wants an answer before then. The sacristan, you are warned, says little.

**Epilogue.** The sacristan has been selling the silver of the relics for years, and Vesper saw him. In the rosary, in a hollow bead: «The noblewoman was waiting for an audience in front of the throne». Around the king's bier, the children who remain are fewer and fewer.

### Case 7 — The Kitchens · 7×7
- **Victim**: Vidar, 22, a cook.
- **Scene**: the castle kitchens. Objects: cauldron, well, barrels, fireplace.
- **Culprit**: Agnes, the pantry-keeper. **Motive**: jealousy; Vidar had been made the king's cook in her place.
- **Other suspects** (6 in all): Thomas (scullion), Laurence (kitchen master), Zachary (butcher), Nicholas (baker), Giles (stableman).
- **Object**: a silver spoon with a hollow handle.

**Introduction.** A cook has died in the castle kitchens, beside the cauldron. The queen receives you in the hall, offers you a cup of wine and drinks first: «No one is to be trusted here, not even cooks». Outside, the castle already knows an investigation is underway.

**Epilogue.** The pantry-keeper breaks: Vidar's promotion had burned inside her. In the handle of the silver spoon, a slip: «The chancellor was writing in the anteroom, near the candelabrum». Seven of the king's children dead: you wonder who, at court, gains from it.

### Case 8 — The Prison Tower · 7×7
- **Victim**: Vivian, 20, a jailer.
- **Scene**: the tower. Objects: cage, armor, sarcophagus (cell), candelabrum.
- **Culprit**: Manfred, captain of the guard. **Motive**: Vivian knew he let prisoners escape for pay.
- **Other suspects** (6 in all): Sweyn (guard), Alwin (thief in the cell), Constance (lady), Tobias (herald), Norman (executioner).
- **Object**: a ring of keys.

**Introduction.** A jailer lies at the bottom of the prison tower, the keys gone. The queen sees you are tired and takes your hands: «The pretenders are almost finished,» she says softly. And she repeats it, as if to convince herself.

**Epilogue.** The captain of the guard names the buyer of the escapes and says no more. In the ring of keys there is a slip that seems harmless: «The queen prayed in the chapel before supper». You put it in your pocket without a second thought.

### Case 9 — The Heir's Feast · 8×8
- **Victim**: Valdemar, 23, the eldest, hailed as heir at the feast.
- **Scene**: the great hall. Objects: throne, banners, candelabra, benches.
- **Culprit**: Lord Fulk of Graywater, a noble of another house. **Motive**: to prevent a coronation and stay at court.
- **Other suspects** (7 in all): herald, jester, bard, countess, knight, priest.
- **Object**: the king's signet ring.

**Introduction.** In the great hall, at the feast, the nobles have just hailed Valdemar as heir when he collapses among the candelabra. Before the empty throne, the queen weeps. You turn, and for a moment she smiles.

**Epilogue.** Lord Fulk confesses: he wanted to avoid a coronation that would have shut him out. Under the bezel of the signet ring you find the last slip: «The king slept in the bed by the window; the one by the fireplace was the queen's». You have nine testimonies and a room to reconstruct.

### Case 10 — The King's Night · 9×9
- **Victim**: King Victor.
- **Scene**: Hollowmere Keep on the night of the death. Suggested rooms: marital chamber (two beds, fireplace, chest), anteroom, chapel, kitchens, council hall, courtyard, pantry, corridor.
- **Culprit**: Queen Adela. **Motive**: the betrayal. **Means**: foxglove in the evening cup. **Accomplice**: Eustace, bought for the report.
- **Hidden clues**: the nine from cases 1-9.

**Introduction.** You hold nine slips, a king dead in his bed, and the person who chose you standing at the door of the marital chamber. «Retrace that night with me,» says Adela. You already know someone has lied.

**Epilogue.** The picture is complete: only one person could have been left alone with the king. Before you stands the woman who put the inquiry in your hands, and who for months prepared the cup. Now it is up to you to decide what to do with her.

## 6. The two endings

After case 10 the player chooses between two epilogues. Both are bitter.

**Arrest.** *Button*: «I accuse the queen». Adela confesses without raising her voice: she gave the realm a king for thirty-four years and was given twenty-four years of betrayal in return. She is tried and condemned. The throne stays empty, the nine children are dead, and a council of regents swears to rule in the name of a kingdom without a king. You remain the investigator of a court with no one left to protect.

**Mercy.** *Button*: «I stay silent». You put the last slip in the fire, Adela abdicates and retires to a convent of silence. The false report stays in the registers and the truth dies with the two of you. The realm has one mourning less and one secret more, and at every dinner you attend you know that somebody at Hollowmere is raising a glass to a dead man who was never avenged.

## 7. Consistency rules for writing the cases

- **No V among the suspects** (only the king and his children). Within one case, all initials differ.
- **No magic**: no wizards, runes, crystal balls or creatures. Objects are earthly ones (anvil, cauldron, altar, sarcophagus, cage...).
- **Difficulty**: grids of 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, then 9×9. An N×N grid has N people: **N−1 suspects plus the victim** (4×4 = 3 suspects, 9×9 = 8). The personal clues needed for a unique solution grow quickly (about 4-5 for the first cases, 12-14 for the finale).
- **Spoilers**: the case files (`.murdoku.json`) are public and contain the solution; do not put the culprit's name in the title or the briefing.
- **Texts in the app**: campaign title «The King Is Dead»; case labels «1. The Smith's Apprentice», «2. The Abbey Garden»... up to «10. The King's Night»; description: «King Victor of Carvenna has been found dead in his bed. Everyone says his heart gave out; no one believes it. The queen has chosen you as court investigator: find the truth, wherever it leads.»
